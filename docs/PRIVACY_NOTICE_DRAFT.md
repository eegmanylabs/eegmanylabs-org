## What we hold about you

Your **profile** holds your name, your affiliations, institution and laboratory, your country, your ORCID iD if you have one, links to your scholarly or laboratory pages, your photograph's file name if you appear on the public website, and your roles in the programme and its replications. Other signed-in members can see your profile.

Your **contact details** are your email addresses and, if we have it, your postal address. These are kept separately from your profile and only a few people can see them, as explained below.

Your **account** is the login you use to sign in. It records your email address, how you sign in, whether you have set up a second sign-in step, and when you last signed in.

Your **work in the portal** includes the pages, comments, tasks, progress updates, recruitment reports, dataset records and authorship details you add or are named in. Every change records who made it and when, so that the record of the programme's work is reliable.

Programme administrators can keep **private notes** about members, which only they can read.

**Gender and ethnicity** are held only if you give your explicit consent. If you do not consent, these stay blank. You give, review and withdraw your consent on your Account page, which shows the wording you agree to and keeps a record of each time you gave or withdrew it. While your consent is in force, the portal holds the terms that the programme's membership workbook recorded for you, if it recorded any, and anything you enter or confirm there in your own words. When you withdraw, your values and every copy the portal kept of them are deleted at once, and they are deleted again if a backup made before your withdrawal is ever restored. Withdrawing is as easy as giving consent.

If you were contacted by the programme about joining a replication, we may hold a record of that contact: your name, email address, when you were contacted, your reply and any notes made at the time. This applies whether or not you joined.

The portal does not hold EEG recordings or information that identifies the participants in your studies. It records where datasets are stored and how many participants were recruited.

## Where your information comes from

Most profiles were created before their owners were invited, from the programme's own records: the public website, the list of studies, the naming conventions, the recruitment and contact workbook, the membership and contributor workbook, contributor sheets for manuscripts, and the programme's task-management service. When you first sign in, the portal matches your verified email address to your profile so that you can take it over. After that, you can correct and add to your profile yourself.

## Why we use your information, and on what legal basis

We use your information to run the #EEGManyLabs programme: to know who takes part and in what role, to let replication and laboratory teams work together, to track recruitment and datasets, to keep an accurate record of scientific decisions and of who contributed to each publication, and to publish information you have agreed to on the public website.

Our legal basis for this is that it is necessary for a task carried out in the public interest (Article 6(1)(e) of the UK GDPR). **[THE TASK AND ITS BASIS IN LAW TO BE STATED ONCE CONFIRMED BY THE DATA PROTECTION OFFICER.]**

We hold gender and ethnicity only on the basis of your explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a) of the UK GDPR). We use them to produce overall figures about the make-up of the programme, and nobody can see a figure for a group of fewer than five people. A person to whom a programme administrator has given a named, time-limited permission can also see the values of one member at a time, for example to check a record, and every such look is recorded.

You do not have to give us gender or ethnicity information, and nothing in the portal depends on it.

## Companies that handle your information for us

Google runs the services the portal is built on. Your profile, contact details, work in the portal and any gender and ethnicity information are stored by Google in London.

Your **account** is handled by Firebase Authentication, a Google service that processes data only in the United States. This means your email address, your sign-in details, the internet (IP) address and browser you sign in from, and your second sign-in step are processed in the United States. Gender and ethnicity never go there. **[THE SAFEGUARD THAT COVERS THIS TRANSFER TO BE STATED ONCE CONFIRMED BY THE DATA PROTECTION OFFICER, WITH HOW TO OBTAIN A COPY.]**

The portal's pages are delivered by GitHub Pages. GitHub sees the request for the pages and records your IP address for security purposes. Your information inside the portal travels directly between your browser and Google and does not pass through GitHub.

The portal may use a Google service called reCAPTCHA to check that requests come from the genuine portal. When that check is switched on, your browser sends signals to Google for this purpose.

## Emails

The portal does not send you notification emails for now. Notifications about mentions, tasks and decisions appear only inside the portal. The only emails you will receive from the portal are the single-use sign-in links you ask for, which Firebase Authentication sends.

## Artificial intelligence and automated decisions

The portal has no artificial intelligence features and does not send your information to any AI service.

When you first sign in, the portal links you to your profile automatically if exactly one unclaimed profile has your verified email address. If the match is uncertain, a programme administrator decides. Roles that carry management powers, such as leading a replication, are always confirmed by a programme administrator. If you think you have been matched wrongly, you can tell us and a person will review it.

## Information kept in your browser

When you sign in, your browser keeps the information it needs to keep you signed in. The portal also keeps a copy of some of the records you can see in your browser so that it works when your connection drops. Gender and ethnicity are never kept in your browser. If you use a shared computer, sign out when you finish. You can also end all your sessions on every device from your account page.

The portal has no advertising or analytics. Its own code is delivered with the portal. The only code it loads from elsewhere is Google's, for signing in with Google and for the reCAPTCHA check, and only when those are switched on.

## Your rights

You can ask for a **copy** of everything the portal holds about you. A programme administrator produces it as a single file, which includes the record of your consent to holding gender and ethnicity.

You can ask us to **correct** your information. You can edit much of your profile yourself, and for information that came from the programme's records you can send a correction request from the portal.

You can ask us to **delete** your information. Because we rely on a task in the public interest, the legal right to deletion is limited, and you can still ask. Two programme administrators deal with each request: one makes the request and a second decides it. Deleted information stays in backups until they expire, as listed above, and is never brought back from them.

You can **object** to our use of your information. We will stop unless we have compelling legitimate grounds to continue that outweigh your interests, or need the information for legal claims.

If you have given consent for gender and ethnicity, you can **withdraw** it at any time on your Account page. Withdrawing deletes the values at once and does not affect our use of the information before you withdrew.

To use any of these rights, contact us at **[CONTACT DETAILS TO BE COMPLETED BY THE PROGRAMME]**.
